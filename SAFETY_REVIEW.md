# Safety review: existing six-hour protocol

**Status: NOT VALIDATED — DO NOT USE AS EMERGENCY, FORAGING, FOOD-SAFETY OR SURVIVAL INSTRUCTIONS.**

This review identifies material safety problems in the existing README and application content. It is a stop-use warning, not a replacement field manual. The existing protocol has not been validated for a specific coast, species, jurisdiction, season or user capability.

## High-risk claims that must not be followed as written

- **Unidentified wild foods:** generic colour-based seaweed suggestions and informal “chew tests” cannot reliably identify edible species or exclude toxins, contamination or harmful reactions.
- **Shellfish:** smell, shell closure and appearance do not reliably exclude marine biotoxins, sewage contamination or other hazards. Cooking does not reliably destroy all marine biotoxins.
- **Raw fish / ceviche:** acidic juice does not make raw fish reliably safe from parasites or pathogens. Do not present it as a cooking or emergency-safety method.
- **Sun-heated rocks:** sunlight on rocks is not a validated cooking method and cannot be relied upon to reach a safe internal food temperature.
- **Improvised blades and fire:** stone-chipping, improvised cutting tools, and heating rocks can cause serious injury; some rocks can fracture or explode when heated.
- **Tide pools, surf and rocks:** the protocol may encourage entering hazardous coastal areas, where tides, waves, weather and slippery terrain can rapidly become life-threatening.
- **Water:** seaweed moisture is not a drinking-water plan. This document does not establish a safe source or validated purification method.
- **Legal and ecological conditions:** collection, fishing, protected areas, species restrictions and local contamination advisories vary by place and time. Generic advice cannot establish legality or safety.

## Required product changes before public use

1. Keep this warning prominent wherever the old protocol is displayed or linked.
2. Do not market the current app or README as a validated survival guide, emergency system, or guaranteed way to obtain food.
3. Replace executable foraging and food-handling instructions only with content reviewed against authoritative local public-health, marine-safety, food-safety and legal sources.
4. Attach a jurisdiction, source URL, publication/update date, evidence status and review owner to every safety-critical instruction.
5. Make emergency advice prioritise moving away from immediate hazards and contacting local emergency services when possible; do not send readers into water or onto dangerous terrain to obtain food.
6. Check the application prompt and data separately: a README warning does not neutralise unsafe instructions still rendered in the app or generated by its AI assistant.
7. Do not claim the app is free to operate: its current package includes a hosted AI SDK and its setup references an API key. API access may have limits or charges; verify provider terms before use.

## Release gate

The current survival-oriented material must remain labelled **unvalidated** until reviewed. Do not publish it as a reliable emergency guide or sell it as safety-critical instruction. A safer first sellable direction may be a non-emergency educational printable about coastal observation, tide awareness and marine conservation, with local context and sourced references—not a foraging manual.
